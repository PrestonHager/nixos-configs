Brand & style guide

Night sky, warm light

The lab's machines are named after stars and constellations, so the brand is a night sky with one bright star over home. Calm, technical and a little playful. This page is the reference for anything that carries the Hager Homelab name.

Logo

The mark is a constellation drawn as a house: four stars for the walls, a door, and a four-point guiding star at the roof's peak. The house is home; the stars are the servers; the gold star is the one login that ties it all together.

Name

Write it Hager Homelab: two words, title case. In the wordmark, "Hager" is bold and "Homelab" is medium weight in a muted tone. Short form, where space is tight: the lab. Never "HagerLab", "HHL" or "Hager Home Lab".

Logo usage

  • Clear space: keep empty space around the logo equal to the height of the guiding star (about ¼ of the mark's height).
  • Minimum size: mark 16 px (favicon), lockup 120 px wide on screen.
  • Backgrounds: full-color logo on Night 950–800. On light or busy backgrounds use the monochrome mark in Night 900 or white.

Do

  • Use the supplied SVGs.
  • Keep the gold star gold in full-color use.
  • Scale proportionally.

Don't

  • Recolor the lines or stars outside the palette.
  • Add shadows, glows, outlines or gradients.
  • Rotate, stretch or re-draw the house.
  • Put the full-color logo on photos or mid-tones.

Color

A dark-first palette. Night is the canvas, Starlight is the single warm accent for primary actions and highlights, and Aurora marks secure or interactive things: links, focus rings, VPN paths. Use gold sparingly: one primary action per view.

Typography

Fast and private: the site ships no web fonts. It uses each platform's best UI face (Inter if it's installed, otherwise Segoe UI, San Francisco or Roboto) and the system monospace for code, labels and eyebrows. Headings are bold with tight tracking; mono labels are uppercase with wide tracking.

Components

Buttons

Primary action Secondary

One primary (Starlight) button per view. Labels are verbs: "Open the member guide", not "Click here".

Callouts

Info

Aurora border: tips and context.

Heads up

Starlight border: something to do before continuing.

Danger

Ember border: security or data loss. Use rarely.

Cards & tags

Card title

Raised surface, 14 px radius, 1 px border and a soft shadow. Content first; decoration minimal.

tagmono label

Shape & spacing

  • Radii: 8 px (inputs, code), 14 px (cards), 22 px (feature panels), fully round (tags).
  • Spacing follows a 4 px grid; page gutters are 16–32 px; content max width 1120 px, prose 760 px.
  • Motion is small and quick (≤ 150 ms lifts and fades) and is turned off for prefers-reduced-motion.

Icons & imagery

  • Icons: 24 px outline icons, 2 px stroke, round caps and joins (Lucide-style). Tint with Aurora inside a soft Aurora tile.
  • Illustrations: constellations: dots joined by thin lines, one gold star for emphasis. Flat, no gradients except a faint background glow.
  • Diagrams: rounded boxes, solid grey wires for public traffic, dashed Aurora wires for member/VPN traffic, mono uppercase zone labels.
  • Photography: avoid photos of real hardware, racks, labels or screens. They leak more than you'd think.

Voice & tone

We sound like

  • A friend who runs servers for fun: plain words, short sentences.
  • Specific about what to do, vague about what's inside.
  • Honest about limits ("invite-only", "VPN required").
  • Occasionally playful (one emoji per page, tops).

We avoid

  • Marketing fluff ("enterprise-grade", "cutting-edge").
  • Internal names, addresses, ports, hardware models or network layouts on public pages.
  • Blame in error text. Say what happened and what to try next.

Public vs. member content: public pages describe the lab's shape (layers, principles, upstream projects). Anything that would help someone map or attack it (hostnames of servers, IP ranges, versions, topology) belongs in the VPN-only member docs.

Design tokens

All colors are CSS custom properties on :root in style.css. Components use the semantic tokens, never raw hex, so light and dark themes come for free.
